# EMPRESS: CINEMATIC

A four minute procedural cinematic, cut to its soundtrack, rendered in real time
in the browser with WebGL2. No level data, no models, no textures, no runtime
libraries. Every frame is generated by the code in this repository.

**Live: https://omniscye.github.io/empress-kingdom-hearts-cinematic/**

Press PLAY on the loading screen. That is the whole interface.

---

## What this is

The renderer underneath is a deferred WebGL2 pipeline originally built to fly an
endless procedural neon city: a G-buffer, clustered emissive light volumes,
screen space reflections, volumetric scatter, a progressive bloom chain and an
ACES tone mapper. This build keeps that engine and drives it as a film camera
instead of a drone.

The city flight itself is not part of this build. One page, one renderer, one
thing to press: a cut, scored, ten sequence film, 4:23 long.

---

## The film

The soundtrack was analysed offline before a single frame was authored: a 2048
sample STFT at 512 hop, spectral flux onset detection, a comb filter tempo
search across 120 to 175 BPM at 0.05 BPM resolution, phase locked over the whole
take, downbeats picked by bass flux across the four beat positions, and per band
envelopes resampled to 30 Hz. The result lives in `src/cinematic/analysis.js`.

```
duration   263.498 s
tempo      145.35 BPM, 0.412797 s per beat
metre      4/4, first downbeat at 0.156 s
grid       638 beats, 160 bars
onsets     182 detected transients
```

Every sequence boundary below is a real position in that recording. Most are
downbeats of the grid. Two are detected transients, because the music does

## How it works

### One timeline, one number

The whole film is a pure function of the clock. Sequences, camera shots,
keyframed parameters, transitions and impacts all resolve from the current time,
which is why the scrubber holds together, why a restart is identical, and why the
offline render is reproducible frame for frame.

`src/cinematic/timeline.js` carries three kinds of thing:

* **tracks**, keyframed values with easing, pure functions of t
* **cues**, ranged events with a 0 to 1 progress, pure functions of t
* **triggers**, one shot impulses fired on a forward crossing, used only to seed
  stateful systems and always paired with a decaying cue so a scrub into the
  middle of one still looks right

### The camera

`src/cinematic/cameraPaths.js` is a film camera, not a drone. It holds
keyframes, rides Catmull-Rom splines, orbits, tracks moving subjects, whips,
shakes, breathes on the lens, and above all cuts. Consecutive shots are a hard
cut unless the later one asks for a blend.

```js
const s = rig.shot(12.0, 4.0, { spline: true, ease: 'inOutSine' });
s.keyframe(0, [0, 2, 10], [0, 2, 0], 50);
s.keyframe(4, [4, 3,  6], [0, 2, 0], 38);
```

Shot types are `keys`, `orbit`, `track`, `dolly` and `custom`.

Nothing in the music moves the camera. The camera is authored, the shots are
authored, and the handful of shocks that do exist are placed by hand at specific
moments and evaluated as decaying functions of time, so landing in the middle of
a hit still shows the tail of it. A camera that trembles on every kick reads as a
loose tripod rather than as an edit, and a scrub back to the same second has to
produce the same frame.

A cut is the only thing allowed to move the frame instantly. Scrubbing settles
the scene before the frame is drawn: the director steps itself a few frames at
the new time, so shots that track a subject already know where the subject is
and cannot snap on the first frame after a jump.

### The cast

`src/cinematic/characters.js`. A figure is fifteen joints in character space, a
set of capsules stretched between them, spiked hair, an optional cape, an
optional key shaped blade, and a rim light. Poses are joint **positions** rather
than joint angles, which makes them trivially blendable and impossible to get
into the broken states a naive Euler chain falls into.

`createStandingPose`, `createFallingPose`, `createRunningPose`,
`createAttackPose`, `createFloatingPose`, `createDefensivePose`,
`createReachingPose`, `createStruckPose`, `createKneelingPose`.

### Surfaces

Everything the film draws into the G-buffer goes through one program with a
`kind` selector, and each kind branches into a procedural surface. There are no
image assets anywhere. The stained glass platform is sixteen ring segments
running a polar pattern shader, so the mandala is generated, continuous, and
different at every radius. Memory panels carry an image that never quite
resolves: a horizon, drifting cloud, figures suggested by noise, a frame, a scan
line and the occasional dropout.

`MATTE`, `GLASS`, `ENERGY`, `SILHOUETTE`, `MEMORY`, `SHARD`, `STONE`,
`STATION`, `BLADE`, `VOIDGLASS`, `HOLO`, `TUBE`.

### Particles, two ways

A **pool** for events: bursts, sparks, embers off a blade. Simulated, stepped at
a fixed dt during a render so the result is reproducible.

**Fields** for atmosphere: a lattice of cells around the camera where every cell
hashes to a fixed offset, colour and drift. Not simulated at all, so the field is
infinite, costs no memory, never needs seeding, and is identical on every
machine. Same trick the city uses to make an endless world out of a hash.

### The break

At bar 79 the frame that was on screen a moment ago is held, cracked along a
polar lattice of about 250 shards, and thrown at the lens. The lattice is static
geometry animated entirely in the vertex shader from each shard's own id, so the
whole break is one draw call with no per frame CPU work. Three dimensional debris
is emitted at the same instant so the pieces still exist once the plate has gone.

### Post

On top of the city's bloom, SSR, scatter, ACES, aberration and grain:

* depth of field, half resolution golden angle bokeh, weighted so a sharp
  foreground cannot bleed onto a blurred background
* camera motion blur, reprojected through the previous frame's view projection
* radial rush and procedural speed lines
* the glass break
* a transition deck: crossfade, radial wipe, iris, angular sweep, particle
  dissolve, bar wipe, bloom out, fade, flash, darkness
* halation, gate weave, letterbox and title cards rasterised from system fonts

### Rim light

A rim light is a geometric edge, not a grazing angle. Judging it by view angle
alone puts a full strength rim across every large plate the camera looks along,
which turns a mirror floor into a sheet of white. The ambient pass finds the
actual silhouette with a depth discontinuity test, so the rim lands where an eye
expects it: on the outline of a figure against whatever is behind it.

### What the music actually drives

The score does not push the camera, does not throw rings across the floor, and
does not pulse anything on the beat. What it does is colour.

Bass warms the grade and the rim light on everything already standing in the
shot. Treble cools it. Overall energy opens the exposure by a percent or two.
The result is that a heavy bar looks amber on the figures and the architecture
that are on screen anyway, and a bright one turns the same surfaces blue, so the
picture moves with the track without any part of the picture existing purely to
move with the track.

### Exposure

Each sequence carries a measured exposure trim. They were not guessed. Each
passage was rendered across an exposure sweep and the multiplier that lands its
average frame in the intended part of the curve was read off the result.

## Rendering it out

Press RENDER, or `E`.

**Frame sequence.** Offline and deterministic. The clock is frame index divided
by frame rate, dt is exactly one frame, the resolution is pinned to whatever you
asked for, and the GPU is allowed to take as long as it needs per frame. Frames
go straight to a folder you pick through the File System Access API where the
browser has it, and to a series of plain stored zips where it does not. The
soundtrack and an `ASSEMBLE.txt` with a ready made ffmpeg line land beside them.

```
ffmpeg -framerate 30 -start_number 0 -i frame_%06d.jpg \
       -i soundtrack.mp3 \
       -c:v libx264 -preset slow -crf 16 -pix_fmt yuv420p \
       -c:a aac -b:a 320k -shortest \
       empress-kingdom-hearts-cinematic.mp4
```

Presets: 1920x1080 at 30 or 60, 2560x1440, 3840x2160, 1280x720. Frames as JPEG
or lossless PNG. Any time range.

**Real time capture.** One click, records what the preview shows with the score
muxed in through MediaRecorder, and hands you a playable webm. Convenient rather
than exact: frame timing is at the mercy of the machine.

**Still.** Saves the current frame as a PNG at the chosen resolution.

## Quality

| Tier | For |
| --- | --- |
| Performance | Weaker hardware, and scrubbing the timeline |
| High | The intended look at a comfortable frame rate |
| Ultra | Everything on, expect a strong GPU |
| Film | For rendering out, not meant to hold real time |

The tier sets frame store capacities, cast and crowd sizes, shard, panel and
island counts, beam counts, and the sample counts for focus, motion blur, radial
blur, occlusion, reflections and scatter. Nothing allocates during playback: the
whole cast, the whole particle field and every light are written into arrays that
were sized at boot and are simply rewound each frame.

## Requirements

WebGL2 with hardware acceleration and `EXT_color_buffer_float`, which is any
current desktop browser and most recent mobile ones. No build step, no bundler,
no package manager, no server. Clone it and open `index.html` through any static
file server.

One audio track. Nothing else is imported.

| File | Track | Source |

The soundtrack is included for playback with this project only. If you fork
this, swap it for something you have the rights to and re-run the analysis.

generated by code in this repository, and the interface uses whichever faces the
visitor's system already provides.
